# PitchBlack Client Onboarding Script

**Last Updated:** 2026-04-17
**Purpose:** PitchBlack-facing sales narrative for qualified prospects considering Growth-tier engagement. Frames the Bakery Snapshot (and future sibling snapshots) as done-for-you operational infrastructure — not marketing, not design, not generic consulting. Positions PitchBlack against the "marketing agency" category that prospects will naturally compare it to.
**Companion asset:** [PITCHBLACK_INTAKE_QUESTIONNAIRE.md](PITCHBLACK_INTAKE_QUESTIONNAIRE.md) — sent to prospects as the next step after script walkthrough.
**Deployment format:** 1-page PDF OR spoken narrative script OR Loom video voiceover. Adapt copy to medium.

## The script

### Opening hook (30-45 seconds)

> Most small businesses we talk to have the same problem — and it's not a marketing problem, even though they sometimes think it is.
>
> It's this: the leads and inquiries and follow-ups and customer data they already have are scattered across five or six different tools that don't talk to each other. Email is in one place. SMS is in a personal phone. Catering inquiries land in an email inbox that gets checked when someone remembers. Google reviews are "I'll ask later." The POS knows one thing about the customer; the spreadsheet knows another; the Mailchimp list knows a third. Nothing is wrong individually — but the seams between tools are where leads die, reviews don't happen, and wholesale accounts go quiet without anyone noticing.
>
> We don't fix that by adding another marketing tool. We fix it by installing one operational system where all of that runs in one place, automatically, whether you're at the market, sleeping, or baking.

### What gets installed (60-90 seconds)

> Here's what actually gets built in your business. Plain language, no jargon:
>
> **A unified customer database.** Every customer — market regular, wholesale client, catering inquiry, Google review-leaver — lives in one place. Not five places. One. Tagged by where they came from, what they've bought, what they care about (gluten-free? wholesale account? wedding inquiry?). Your whole operation sees the same truth.
>
> **Automated follow-up for every type of customer.** New lead fills out your form → welcomed within 15 minutes. Catering inquiry arrives → booking link sent, tasting scheduled, quote auto-tracked, deposit workflow runs itself. Wholesale prospect goes quiet → system nudges them. Customer hasn't shown up at the market in two months → personal "we miss you" email fires. You don't have to remember any of it.
>
> **Review requests that actually happen.** Every completed catering event, every fulfilled custom order, every appointment — the system asks for a review. Google's ranking algorithm rewards this. Your local SEO compounds. You don't have to ask anyone manually.
>
> **Sales pipelines for the two real revenue motions.** Wholesale onboarding from first sample to recurring order. Catering from inquiry to delivered event. Each pipeline stage has its own automations. Revenue is visible. Stuck deals get surfaced.
>
> **Forms, a chat widget, payment links, invoices, e-sign contracts, gift cards, a booking calendar.** All wired together, all branded to your business, all writing to the same customer database.
>
> **A dashboard** you check in the morning that tells you: who's in the pipeline, what's coming up, what's overdue, and where the money is.

### Timeline expectations (30 seconds)

> From the day you complete our intake questionnaire to the day your system goes live: **two to four weeks.**
>
> Week 1: We set up your domain, email sending, phone number, and payment infrastructure. Some of this has a carrier review window — SMS compliance registration takes three to seven business days, so we start it the moment we have your business details.
>
> Weeks 2-3: We build your customer database schema, the two sales pipelines, the 10-12 automation workflows, your forms, your website or funnel, and your review-request infrastructure.
>
> Week 4: You test it. We train you on it. We hand it off.

### What PitchBlack does vs. what you do (45 seconds)

> Clear division of labor, because this matters.
>
> **We do:**
>
> - All the HighLevel build work. You don't touch the platform except to approve things.
> - All the technical configuration — DNS records, payment integration, SMS compliance, API connections.
> - All the workflow design — every automated email and SMS template, every pipeline stage, every trigger and action.
> - A handover training session with you or whoever will operate the system day-to-day.
> - 30 days of post-launch support for questions, small tweaks, and help interpreting what the system is showing you.
>
> **You do:**
>
> - Answer our intake questionnaire honestly and thoroughly (about 30 minutes of your time).
> - Send us your brand assets — logo, colors, fonts, a paragraph about your brand voice.
> - Connect your existing Stripe account (or let us set one up), claim your Google Business Profile if it isn't already.
> - Operate the system once it's live. Reply to customer messages in the inbox we built you. Update your product catalog when recipes change. Ask us when you get stuck.
>
> What we don't do: we don't run your marketing. We don't write your social posts (though the system drafts them for you). We don't manage paid ads (the system is built to support them if you want to activate that later). We don't answer your customers' messages for you. This is your business; we build the infrastructure you operate.

### Pricing framing (30 seconds)

> Our Growth-tier engagement is priced as a **one-time setup investment plus a monthly recurring platform fee.** The setup covers the build we just walked through — from intake to go-live. The monthly fee covers the HighLevel platform (which you're paying for anyway, just through us instead of direct), plus ongoing access to us for questions, minor adjustments, and the six-month platform-update review that keeps your build current as HighLevel evolves.
>
> We send specific numbers after we've seen your intake questionnaire and understand your scope. We don't quote blind because every business has at least one wrinkle we account for.
>
> One thing we are transparent about: we're not the cheapest option. We're not trying to be. If you want a $99-a-month template, a freelancer on Upwork can do that. What you get from us is a system built by people who understand both the platform and the kind of business you're running, with a warranty and an update path that keeps it from rotting in six months.

### Call to action (15 seconds)

> If what I just described sounds like the operational layer your business is missing, here's the next step: **complete our intake questionnaire.** Link below. It takes about 30 minutes. Be thorough; what you put in determines what we build.
>
> Once we have your questionnaire, we send back a proposal within two business days. If the numbers work, we start the clock. If they don't, no hard feelings — we'll point you to alternatives that might fit better.
>

## Deployment variants

### Variant A — Loom video script (5-7 minutes)

Pace the above script as spoken narrative. Open with founder (Dan) on camera; cut to screen-share of a dashboard / pipeline view / automation builder during the "What gets installed" section. Close on founder again for CTA.

### Variant B — PDF with sales deck styling

Format as a 2-3 page PDF:
- Page 1: Hook + what-gets-installed (text + one visual: the dashboard screenshot or pipeline Kanban).
- Page 2: Timeline + division-of-labor table + pricing framing.
- Page 3: CTA with embedded QR code or link to Intake Questionnaire.

Styled in PitchBlack brand (not client's). Hand to prospect during or after sales conversation.

### Variant C — Email / DM one-pager

Condensed to 300-400 words. Drop Timeline and Division-of-Labor sections. Keep Hook, What-gets-installed (condensed), Pricing framing (sentence only), CTA.

Used for cold outbound or initial-interest-reply when prospect hasn't yet scheduled a call.

### Variant D — Live conversation talking points

Use the structure as a mental outline for founder-led sales calls. Hook → what gets installed → timeline → boundary → pricing → ask them to complete questionnaire. 15-20 minutes conversational; reserves pricing specifics for post-questionnaire.

## What NOT to say in this script

Per our positioning, avoid:

- "**Marketing agency**" / "we do marketing for you." False claim that misprices the engagement and positions us against the wrong competitor set.
- "**Turnkey**." Overused. Prospects have been promised turnkey by every agency that didn't deliver it. Use "done-for-you operational infrastructure" or similar.
- **Specific technology names to the prospect** (HighLevel / Stripe / Twilio). Mention only if they ask. Most small-business prospects don't care what the platform is called; they care what it does.
- **Generic feature lists** ("SMS, email, CRM, automation"). Prospects glaze over. Use concrete workflows: "system asks for a Google review after every completed event."
- "**AI**" as a selling point. Don't lead with it. Content AI is a subcomponent; leading with AI invites skepticism and comparison to fly-by-night AI agencies.
- "**You'll save X hours per week**" unless we have actual data from a prior client. Vague productivity claims read as marketing-speak. Once Caramel Oven delivers 30-day metrics, use those specifics.

## Adaptation log

The script above is calibrated for **small-batch food/beverage small businesses** (farmers-market vendors, boutique coffee, juice bars, artisan bakeries, florists, small catering companies).

For adjacent verticals (home services, wellness, boutique retail), adapt the hook examples:
- Home services: "leads go stale because follow-up is manual"
- Wellness: "client re-booking is ad-hoc; retention drops"
- Boutique retail: "no visibility into which customers are likely to come back"

Pipeline examples shift: wholesale → referral partnerships (home services), reactivation → package renewal (wellness), catering → events or collaborations (boutique retail).

Do NOT use this script as-written for verticals that need a fundamentally different operational model (SaaS, e-commerce, enterprise B2B, medical/legal with heavy compliance overhead). Those need their own script.
