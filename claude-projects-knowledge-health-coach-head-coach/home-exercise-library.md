## Lower Body

### Bodyweight Squat
- **Description:** Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, toes slightly out. Push hips back and bend knees until thighs are at or below parallel. Keep chest up, weight in mid-foot/heels. Stand back up to full hip extension.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 12-15 reps.
- **Regression:** Squat to a chair or bench (box squat) — sit and stand.
- **Progression:** Pause squat (3-second hold at bottom) or add a backpack with weight.

### Reverse Lunge
- **Description:** From standing, step one foot backward and lower the back knee toward the floor until both knees are near 90 degrees. Push through the front heel to return to standing. Alternate legs.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 10 per leg.
- **Regression:** Hold a wall or doorframe for balance; reduce range of motion.
- **Progression:** Deficit reverse lunge (front foot on a low step) or add weight (backpack, gallon jugs).

### Glute Bridge
- **Description:** Lie face-up, knees bent, feet flat on floor hip-width apart. Drive hips toward ceiling by squeezing glutes. Pause at top. Lower with control.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 15 reps.
- **Regression:** Reduce range — don't drive all the way up; hold for shorter duration.
- **Progression:** Single-leg glute bridge (one foot lifted) or elevate feet on a chair.

### Step-Up
- **Description:** Using a sturdy step, chair, or low bench (12-18 inches): place one foot fully on the surface, drive through that foot to stand on top, bring the trailing leg up. Step back down with control. Alternate or do all reps one side then switch.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 8-10 per leg.
- **Regression:** Lower surface height (thick book, bottom stair).
- **Progression:** Higher surface, add weight, or slow the eccentric (3-second lowering).

### Wall Sit
- **Description:** Back flat against a wall, slide down until thighs are parallel to the floor. Knees at 90 degrees, directly above ankles. Hold.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 30-45 seconds.
- **Regression:** Slide less deep (higher hip position).
- **Progression:** Single-leg wall sit or hold weight on lap.

## Upper Body Push

### Push-Up
- **Description:** Hands slightly wider than shoulder-width, body in a straight line from head to heels. Lower chest to the floor (elbows at ~45 degrees from torso), push back up to full arm extension.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 8-12 reps (or as many as possible with good form).
- **Regression:** Incline push-up (hands on a counter, bench, or wall — the higher the hands, the easier).
- **Progression:** Decline push-up (feet elevated), diamond push-up, or weighted push-up (backpack).

### Pike Push-Up
- **Description:** Hips high in an inverted-V position (like downward dog). Hands shoulder-width. Bend elbows and lower the top of your head toward the floor between your hands. Push back up. Targets shoulders.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 6-10 reps.
- **Regression:** Reduce range of motion; keep hips less elevated.
- **Progression:** Feet elevated on a chair (steeper angle) or deficit pike push-up (hands on books for deeper range).

### Dip (Using Two Chairs or Counter Edge)
- **Description:** Hands on two stable surfaces (chair backs, counter edge, parallettes). Support body weight with arms straight. Bend elbows to lower body until upper arms are roughly parallel to floor. Push back up.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 6-10 reps.
- **Regression:** Bench dip (feet on floor, hands on bench behind you, knees bent).
- **Progression:** Straight legs (bench dip), add weight (backpack), or increase depth.

## Upper Body Pull

### Inverted Row (Using a Sturdy Table or Low Bar)
- **Description:** Lie under a sturdy table or low bar. Grip the edge, body straight from head to heels. Pull chest to the edge, squeezing shoulder blades. Lower with control.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 8-12 reps.
- **Regression:** Bend knees (feet closer to under you) to reduce load.
- **Progression:** Elevate feet on a chair, or use a single arm (assisted).

### Doorframe Row (With a Towel)
- **Description:** Loop a towel around a sturdy doorframe hinge-side or grab both sides of an open doorframe. Lean back with straight arms, body angled. Pull chest toward the frame by squeezing shoulder blades. Return with control.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 10-15 reps.
- **Regression:** Stand more upright (less lean angle).
- **Progression:** Increase lean angle (feet closer to door) or single-arm.

### Backpack/Jug Bent-Over Row
- **Description:** Hold a loaded backpack or gallon jug in one hand. Hinge at hips (flat back, ~45-degree torso angle), brace core. Pull the weight to your hip, elbow driving back. Lower with control.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 10-12 per arm.
- **Regression:** Lighter weight, less range of motion.
- **Progression:** Heavier load, pause at top (2-second squeeze), slower eccentric.

### Pull-Up (If Doorframe Bar Available)
- **Description:** Hang from a pull-up bar with palms facing away, hands shoulder-width or slightly wider. Pull body up until chin clears the bar. Lower with control to full arm extension.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of as many as possible (even 1-2 reps is a starting point).
- **Regression:** Negative pull-ups (jump to top, lower slowly over 5 seconds) or band-assisted.
- **Progression:** Weighted pull-up (backpack), L-sit pull-up, or slow tempo.

## Core

### Dead Bug
- **Description:** Lie face-up, arms straight toward ceiling, knees bent at 90 degrees (shins parallel to floor). Extend one arm overhead and the opposite leg out straight simultaneously, keeping lower back pressed into the floor. Return and switch sides.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 8-10 per side.
- **Regression:** Move only the legs (arms stay up) or only the arms (legs stay bent).
- **Progression:** Hold a light weight in hands, or slow the movement (4-second extension).

### Plank
- **Description:** Forearms and toes on the floor, body in a straight line from head to heels. Squeeze glutes, brace abs, breathe normally. Do not let hips sag or pike up.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 30-45 seconds.
- **Regression:** Knees on floor (short-lever plank).
- **Progression:** Extended plank (arms further forward), plank with shoulder taps, or weighted (backpack).

### Bicycle Crunch
- **Description:** Lie face-up, hands behind head (don't pull on neck). Lift shoulders off floor. Drive one knee toward chest while rotating opposite elbow toward that knee. Extend the other leg straight. Alternate in a pedaling motion.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 12-15 per side.
- **Regression:** Feet on the floor, alternate knee drives without full extension.
- **Progression:** Slower tempo (3-second per rep), or hold at rotation for 2 seconds.

### Hollow Body Hold
- **Description:** Lie face-up. Press lower back into floor. Lift shoulders and legs off the floor simultaneously (arms by ears or at sides). Hold — body forms a slight banana shape. The lower the legs, the harder it is.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 20-30 seconds.
- **Regression:** Bend knees, or keep legs higher (less lever).
- **Progression:** Arms overhead, legs lower, or add gentle rocking motion (hollow body rock).

### Side Plank
- **Description:** Lie on one side, forearm on floor under shoulder. Stack feet (or stagger for balance). Lift hips so body forms a straight line. Hold.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 20-30 seconds per side.
- **Regression:** Bottom knee on floor for support.
- **Progression:** Top leg raised, or add hip dips (lower and lift hips for reps).

## Cardio / Conditioning

### Brisk Walking
- **Description:** Walk at a pace that elevates heart rate and breathing but allows you to hold a conversation (Zone 2). Outdoors or treadmill.
- **Starting point:** 30-45 minutes, 4-6 days per week.
- **Regression:** Shorter duration or slower pace.
- **Progression:** Incline walking (hills or treadmill incline), longer duration, or add a weighted vest/backpack.

### Jumping Jacks
- **Description:** Start standing, feet together, arms at sides. Jump feet apart while raising arms overhead. Jump back to start.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 30 seconds (or 20-30 reps).
- **Regression:** Step-out jacks (step one foot at a time instead of jumping).
- **Progression:** Seal jacks (arms clap in front instead of overhead), or longer intervals.

### Burpee
- **Description:** From standing — squat down, place hands on floor, jump or step feet back to push-up position, perform a push-up (optional), jump or step feet forward, stand and jump with arms overhead.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 5-8 reps.
- **Regression:** Remove the push-up; step back/forward instead of jumping.
- **Progression:** Add a tuck jump at the top, or increase speed/reps.

### Mountain Climbers
- **Description:** Start in push-up position. Drive one knee toward chest, then switch rapidly — like running in place horizontally.
- **Starting point:** 3 sets of 20 seconds.
- **Regression:** Slow the pace (controlled alternating knee drives).
- **Progression:** Cross-body mountain climbers (knee to opposite elbow), faster pace, or longer intervals.

### Jump Rope (If Available)
- **Description:** Standard two-foot bounce. Light on the balls of feet, wrists driving the rotation, elbows close to body.
- **Starting point:** 5-10 sets of 30 seconds on / 30 seconds rest.
- **Regression:** Invisible jump rope (mime the motion without the rope to build rhythm).
- **Progression:** Single-leg hops, double-unders, or longer unbroken intervals.

## Assembling a Balanced Weekly Routine

### Principles
- Hit each movement category (lower body, push, pull, core) at least 2x per week.
- Include 3-5 days of some form of cardio/conditioning (walking counts).
- Allow at least 1 full rest day per week.
- Training sessions don't need to be long: 20-40 minutes of focused work is effective.

### Sample 4-Day Split (Beginner-Intermediate)

**Day 1 — Lower Body + Core**
- Bodyweight squat: 3x12
- Reverse lunge: 3x10/leg
- Glute bridge: 3x15
- Dead bug: 3x10/side
- Plank: 3x30-45s

**Day 2 — Upper Push + Cardio**
- Push-up: 3x8-12
- Pike push-up: 3x6-10
- Dip: 3x6-10
- Mountain climbers: 3x20s
- Brisk walk: 20-30 min

**Day 3 — Rest or Light Walk**

**Day 4 — Upper Pull + Core**
- Inverted row: 3x8-12
- Doorframe row: 3x10-15
- Backpack row: 3x10-12/arm
- Hollow body hold: 3x20-30s
- Side plank: 3x20-30s/side

**Day 5 — Full Body Conditioning**
- Step-up: 3x8-10/leg
- Push-up: 2x max reps
- Jumping jacks: 3x30s
- Burpees: 3x5-8
- Wall sit: 3x30-45s

**Days 6-7 — Active rest (walking, mobility, stretching)**

### Progression Strategy
- Add 1-2 reps per set each week until hitting the top of the rep range.
- Once at the top of the range for all sets, progress to the harder variation.
- Increase cardio duration or intensity by ~10% per week.
- Every 4-6 weeks, take a deload week (reduce volume by 50%) to allow recovery.

## Scaling Workout Intensity During Deep Fasts

### Time-Restricted Eating (16:8, 18:6)
- Train normally. Full intensity and volume are fine.
- Prefer training near the end of the fast or within the eating window for best performance.
- Hydrate and take electrolytes before fasted training.

### 24-Hour Fast Days
- Reduce intensity to 60-70% of normal effort.
- Focus on: walking, light bodyweight circuits (lower volume — 2 sets instead of 3), mobility work, yoga.
- Skip HIIT, heavy conditioning, or max-effort sets.
- If energy is very low, walk only. That is enough.

### Extended Fasts (48-72 Hours)
- **Hours 0-24:** Light training acceptable — walking, gentle bodyweight movements, stretching. Keep heart rate under Zone 3.
- **Hours 24-48:** Walking and mobility only. No resistance training.
- **Hours 48-72:** Walking if energy allows. Complete rest is perfectly fine.
- Do NOT train for performance during an extended fast. The goal is to preserve muscle and stay mobile, not build or push limits.
- Resume normal training only after completing refeed (minimum 12-24 hours of eating).
- Expect the first 1-2 sessions back to feel weaker. This is temporary.

### General Rules for Fasted Training
- Always hydrate and take electrolytes before any fasted movement.
- If dizzy at any point, stop immediately. Sit down, take salt, drink water.
- The deeper the fast, the lower the intensity ceiling.
- Walking is always appropriate regardless of fasting state.
- Never sacrifice form for reps when energy is compromised.
