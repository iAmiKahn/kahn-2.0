# Fasting Protocols

Last verified: 2026-05-16

> **Disclaimer:** This file contains general informational content, not medical advice. Consult a physician or registered dietitian before extended fasting or major dietary change, especially with any pre-existing condition or medication.

---

## Types of Fasting

### Time-Restricted Eating (TRE)
- Eating window limited to a portion of the day; fasting the remainder.
- Common patterns: 16:8 (16 hours fasted, 8-hour eating window), 18:6, 20:4.
- Typical starting point: 16:8. Compress gradually if adapting well.
- Most sustainable for daily use and body recomposition alongside training.

### 24-Hour Fast (One Meal a Day / OMAD)
- Full 24 hours between meals — e.g., dinner to dinner.
- Can be done 1-3 times per week or daily depending on goals and tolerance.
- Useful as a step-up from TRE before attempting extended fasts.

### Extended Fast (48-72 Hours)
- 48-72 hours with zero caloric intake; water, electrolytes, and non-caloric beverages only.
- Deeper autophagy benefits reported beyond 36 hours, peaking around 48-72 hours.
- Should not be attempted without prior experience with shorter fasts.
- Requires electrolyte management (see dedicated section below).
- Should be limited in frequency — typical guidance is no more than once per month for 72-hour fasts, adjusted based on body composition and recovery.

---

## Who Should NOT Fast

Fasting is not appropriate for everyone. Do not fast (or consult a physician first) if any of the following apply:

- Pregnant or breastfeeding
- History of eating disorders (anorexia, bulimia, binge eating disorder)
- Type 1 diabetes or insulin-dependent Type 2 diabetes without medical supervision
- Currently on medications that require food (confirm with prescribing physician)
- BMI under 18.5 / clinically underweight
- Under 18 years old
- Active infection, illness, or recovering from surgery
- History of gout (fasting can spike uric acid)
- Diagnosed adrenal insufficiency or severe chronic fatigue

When in doubt, get medical clearance first.

## How to Prepare for an Extended Fast (48-72 Hours)

### 3-5 Days Before
- Reduce carbohydrate intake gradually (this eases the metabolic transition).
- Increase water intake to ensure you start well-hydrated.
- Stock electrolytes: sodium (salt), potassium (lite salt / potassium chloride), magnesium (citrate or glycinate).
- Plan a low-intensity activity schedule for the fasting days.
- Clear your calendar of high-stress obligations if possible.

### Day Before
- Eat a normal, balanced meal — do not binge or "carb load."
- Avoid alcohol (depletes electrolytes, disrupts sleep, slows adaptation).
- Confirm you have: electrolytes, water, herbal tea, black coffee available.

### Mental Preparation
- Set a clear intention and end time. Know your "why."
- Hunger comes in waves — it peaks and passes. The first 24 hours are typically hardest.
- Have a plan for what to do when cravings hit (walk, cold water, task switch).

## What May Be Consumed During a Fast

### Does NOT Break a Fast
- Water (still or sparkling)
- Black coffee (no cream, sugar, or sweeteners with caloric content)
- Plain tea (black, green, herbal — no milk or sugar)
- Electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium — in water without added sugars)
- Apple cider vinegar (1-2 tbsp in water)
- A small pinch of salt under the tongue for acute lightheadedness

### Breaks a Fast
- Any caloric food or beverage
- Cream, milk, butter in coffee
- Bone broth (contains calories — debated; technically breaks a strict fast but is used in some "modified" protocols)
- Artificial sweeteners (debated — some may trigger insulin; conservative approach is to avoid)
- Supplements with caloric fillers (check labels)
- Gum (most contains small calories and sweeteners)

### Gray Zone (Context-Dependent)
- Bone broth: breaks a strict fast but is sometimes used in modified fasts for electrolytes. If the goal is maximum autophagy, avoid. If the goal is survival on a longer fast, acceptable as a tool.
- Zero-calorie electrolyte drinks: acceptable if truly zero-calorie and no insulin-spiking sweeteners.

## Electrolyte Guidance During Extended Fasts

Electrolyte depletion is the primary physical risk of extended fasting. The body excretes more sodium, potassium, and magnesium when insulin levels drop. Supplementation is not optional during fasts beyond 24 hours.

### Sodium
- **Why it matters:** Regulates blood pressure, fluid balance, nerve function. Lost rapidly during fasting as kidneys excrete more water and sodium.
- **Signs of deficiency:** Headache, dizziness on standing, fatigue, nausea, brain fog, muscle cramps.
- **Typical starting amount:** 2,000-4,000 mg per day (approximately 1-2 teaspoons of salt dissolved in water throughout the day). Adjust based on body size and activity level.
- **Sources:** Table salt, Himalayan pink salt, sole water, bouillon cubes (check calories).

### Potassium
- **Why it matters:** Critical for heart rhythm, muscle contractions, nerve signaling. Deficiency can cause dangerous cardiac arrhythmias.
- **Signs of deficiency:** Muscle weakness, cramps, irregular heartbeat, numbness/tingling, constipation.
- **Typical starting amount:** 1,000-2,000 mg per day. Do NOT megadose — excess potassium is as dangerous as deficiency.
- **Sources:** Lite salt (potassium chloride / sodium chloride blend — e.g., Morton Lite Salt), cream of tartar (1 tsp = ~495 mg potassium), potassium citrate capsules.
- **Caution:** If on ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or potassium-sparing diuretics, consult a physician before supplementing potassium.

### Magnesium
- **Why it matters:** Involved in 300+ enzymatic reactions. Supports muscle relaxation, sleep quality, heart rhythm, and stress regulation.
- **Signs of deficiency:** Muscle twitching/cramps (especially at night), difficulty sleeping, anxiety, constipation, eye twitches.
- **Typical starting amount:** 200-400 mg per day (elemental magnesium).
- **Best forms:** Magnesium glycinate (well-absorbed, calming, less GI upset) or magnesium citrate (well-absorbed, mild laxative effect — useful if constipated).
- **Avoid:** Magnesium oxide (poorly absorbed, mostly acts as a laxative).

### Electrolyte Timing
- Spread intake throughout the day — do not take all at once.
- First dose upon waking. Additional doses mid-morning, afternoon, and evening.
- If feeling lightheaded, take sodium immediately (pinch of salt under tongue or in water).

## Warning Signs — Stop the Fast Immediately

Stop fasting and eat if any of the following occur:

- **Heart palpitations or irregular heartbeat** — may indicate potassium or magnesium imbalance. Eat something with electrolytes and seek medical attention if it persists.
- **Chest pain or pressure** — stop immediately, eat, seek medical attention.
- **Confusion or inability to think clearly** — beyond normal brain fog; if you cannot form sentences or make decisions, eat.
- **Severe dizziness that does not resolve with salt and water** — if sitting/lying down plus sodium does not fix it within 15-20 minutes, break the fast.
- **Fainting or near-fainting**
- **Severe or worsening nausea/vomiting** — makes it impossible to keep electrolytes down; break the fast.
- **Extreme weakness** — if you cannot stand or walk safely.
- **Numbness or tingling that won't resolve** — electrolyte emergency.
- **Severe insomnia lasting more than two consecutive nights** — cortisol may be too elevated; break the fast and recover.

**Rule of thumb:** Mild discomfort is normal. Anything that feels dangerous, scary, or is getting worse rather than passing in waves — stop. You can always fast again later. There is no prize for pushing through warning signs.

## How to Break an Extended Fast Safely

Breaking a fast incorrectly causes GI distress (cramping, diarrhea, nausea) and in rare cases can trigger refeeding syndrome (dangerous electrolyte shifts when insulin spikes after prolonged fasting).

### Refeeding Progression (For 48-72 Hour Fasts)

**Hour 0-2 (First Intake):**
- Small portion. Do not eat a full meal.
- Start with: bone broth, a few bites of soft protein (eggs, fish), or a small handful of nuts.
- Eat slowly. Wait 30-60 minutes before eating more.
- Continue electrolytes.

**Hours 2-6:**
- Add: cooked vegetables, avocado, small portion of protein (chicken, fish, eggs).
- Keep portions moderate. Chew thoroughly.
- Avoid: raw vegetables, dairy, high-sugar foods, large portions.

**Hours 6-12:**
- Can resume more normal eating but still keep portions reasonable.
- Add: cooked starches (rice, potato, sweet potato) in small amounts.
- Continue avoiding: processed foods, sugar, alcohol, large fatty meals.

**After 12-24 Hours:**
- Return to normal eating patterns.
- Note any foods that caused GI distress — avoid those on next refeed.

### Foods to AVOID on Refeed
- Large portions of anything (stomach has shrunk; GI tract has slowed)
- Highly processed or sugary foods (massive insulin spike risk)
- Raw cruciferous vegetables (hard to digest on empty gut)
- Dairy (many people are temporarily more lactose-sensitive post-fast)
- Alcohol (hits extremely hard on an empty/depleted system)
- Nuts in large quantities (calorie-dense, hard to digest in volume)
- Spicy food (GI tract is sensitive)

### Refeeding Syndrome Risk
- Primarily a concern for fasts longer than 5 days or in underweight individuals.
- For 48-72 hour fasts in otherwise healthy, normal-weight individuals, risk is very low but not zero.
- Symptoms: edema, confusion, weakness, cardiac issues in the first 24-48 hours of refeeding.
- Mitigation: break fast with small amounts, continue electrolytes through refeed, eat protein first.

## Adjusting Physical Activity to Fasting State

### Time-Restricted Eating (16:8, 18:6)
- Normal training is fine. Most people can maintain full intensity.
- Train at the end of the fast or early in the eating window for best performance.
- If training fasted, prioritize hydration and electrolytes before/during.

### 24-Hour Fast Days
- Low-to-moderate intensity is fine: walking, light resistance training, yoga, mobility work.
- Avoid maximal-effort or high-volume training.
- Listen to energy levels — if significantly weak, scale back or rest.

### Extended Fasts (48-72 Hours)
- First 24 hours: light activity is fine (walking, stretching).
- Hours 24-48: reduce to walking and gentle mobility only. No resistance training.
- Hours 48-72: walking only if energy allows. Complete rest is acceptable.
- Do NOT attempt heavy lifting, HIIT, or high-heart-rate training during an extended fast. Muscle breakdown risk increases, and electrolyte demand spikes.
- Resume normal training only after completing refeed (12-24 hours of eating).

### General Principles
- Hydrate and take electrolytes before any fasted training.
- If dizzy, stop immediately — sit down, take salt, drink water.
- The goal during a fast is to preserve muscle, not build it. Train to maintain, not to progress.
- Post-fast: expect 1-2 training sessions to feel slightly weaker before returning to baseline. This is normal.
